# Chapter 9: Frozen Frontier

**Nilahima · blue stone · the game that kept them six**

Nilahima was the kind of cold that decides things.

There were cabins with warm doorways. There was a frozen edge of the land, and blue stone under ice the color of old bruises. The family lived close together and spoke little. Their breath hung in the air like cloth.

A tunnel ran under the ice road. It was a brown memory of earth in a white country. They took it because the surface had started to open. Pits hid under the snow. You found them only with your foot. A black snake in the snow took a piece and a day from them. Baby fell into a pit up to his hip. Borin pulled him out with hands that had nothing left to spare.

They were old now, in the way that mattered. It was not only about years. They checked the fire twice. Mani no longer ran into every dark place. Taro's cheer had become a tool he used on purpose, like the copper pin. The pin was worn down to a needle now, and he still carried it.

The blue stone came up in sharp pieces that cut through gloves. Maya traded a moonstone chip for frost salt. Kael made a deal for the right to play with a woman whose face was half scar and half smile.

In the seventh year of the ice, Borin did not get up with the others.

He was breathing. He was not walking. The gentle giant looked at the packs as if they were a joke he finally understood.

"You will have to carry my share," he said to Baby. "But I am still coming."

They did not leave him. They built a sled out of cabin wood and copper nails. Taro cried while he hammered. Maya walked in front to find firm ice. Kael counted their food into smaller and smaller honest shares. Mani pulled until her palms split open. Baby pulled until he could not feel the rope.

Borin saw the cabins of the next fold, and the blue stone in the family's doorway. He saw the gate of Nilahima from inside the Royal Game. They sat him at the board so he could move a piece with one finger. The ice family played as if winning were a way to keep a man alive. On the last roll, Borin whispered a number. Baby rolled it. The piece moved one square, then another, then home.

Afterward Borin slept. In the morning he opened his eyes, slow and angry at the cold, and asked who had won. Baby told him they had. Borin closed his eyes again, smiling. Later that day he sat up and ate.

They did not build a stone grave. Baby tried to put the bent gold shield into Borin's hands. Borin pushed it back. "I am the other shield," he said. "That one still has a job."

Mani stood there until her tears froze. Then she broke them off her face with her thumb.

"Six," she said.

Kael said, "The promise said six. The walk is whoever is left, and we are all still here. That is also a kind of together."

Baby forgave the ice for the ending it had tried to write. He kept the six.

Nine cities won. One crown left. Six walkers, and no empty place at the table.

Kael wrote Borin's name in big letters. He pressed hard so the charcoal would bite deep. It was not a goodbye. It was a fact.

The years of ice taught them to save. Fewer words. Better soup. Taro fixed sleds for the family in return for blue stone chips. Maya drew safe tunnels with charcoal on a bone. Mani hunted seal when hunting was allowed. When it was not, she did not argue.

The black snake had come on the day Baby fell into the pit. It took his piece and left a scale like chipped black glass. Baby kept the scale until Sphatika. Then he gave it back to the ice at the side of the road.

The game with Borin's finger on the piece was the longest hour of Baby's life. Every roll felt like a heartbeat counted out loud. When they won, the ice family did not cheer. They breathed out together. The sound was like wind through a crack. Borin breathed out with them.

Borin asked them to turn his bed toward the south, toward the grass he loved. Mani set his share of the packs where he could see them. "The weight stays," she said. "So do you."

Kael read the whole road so far from his bark. His voice was steady until the last line. There it broke into a laugh he had not planned.

They paid for the blue stone in chips that looked like frozen tears. The ice family gave them furs. They could not refuse the furs without insult. Mani cut hers to fit Baby's shoulders.

The tunnel under the ice road became their last shortcut. Taro marked the entrance with copper nails in a row. There were six. Always six.

When Borin could not walk, Baby told him stories about the scoop at Vardhana and the post at Tamrapatha. Borin fell asleep in the middle of a sentence, smiling.

Somewhere under the ice, something as long as a snake shifted. It was not attacking. It was only waiting for the last gate.

Six walked on. The sled ropes left grooves in Baby's shoulders that never quite faded. Taro talked to his copper needle as he walked. He told it about Borin walking again, about nails, and about bridges that did not make a sound.

Mani broke the ice at the edge of streams so the others could drink. Maya watched the faces of the far crystal peaks and said, "The Crown is close enough to taste." It tasted of cold, and of being almost finished.

Kael carried the bark until Borin could hold a corner of it himself. Kael would not let the sixth walker become a small note at the bottom. The sixth walker refused to be one.

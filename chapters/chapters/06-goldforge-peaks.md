# Chapter 6: Goldforge Peaks

**Suvarnagiri · Gold · The spent life**

Suvarnagiri smoked.

The mountains breathed. A mining camp clung to the rock above veins of gold. The highland clan had already started to talk about a kingdom that would not need ten clans, only one mine and one crown. Smoke stained the snow. Hammers rang like bad weather.

The tunnel here was not a shrine path like the one in Varshana. It was a hole that the miners had left behind, because something in it ate sound. The six used it anyway. Mani went first, then the others. The dark was like a second rain. In that silence, Baby could hear his own blood. Behind it he heard something else, a coil sliding over stone. It was not there yet, but it was coming.

The monster of the Goldforge was not dusk and rust. It was the mine's own night. It drove Mani back down the tunnel, hop after hop, as if a child were playing a cruel game with her body. Baby ran after her with the last shield they had been given. It was a square of beaten gold, no wider than a hand, still warm from the smith's palm.

The shield held. Mani stood up, shaking. The gold square was now a bent coin.

"One life," Kael said quietly. "We have spent it."

They took three measures of gold, because the gate required three. Kael made them swear not to keep a fourth. Maya weighed the ore with her eyes closed, as if listening for a lie in the metal.

The Royal Game was played on a stone table that was still warm from the forges. Heat rose into their elbows. The highland champion played like a man who had already won the century. He was confident and slow, and he smiled at their gray hair and torn packs. Borin sat at this board. He moved each token as if it were a person he refused to leave in the dark. When he blocked the champion's last run, the smiths went quiet.

Afterward, the smiths wanted them to stay and be rich. Borin looked at the peaks and said, "If I stay, I will become the monster. I would rather be tired."

They went down toward the grass. Mani's hands shook for a week. Baby kept the bent gold in his pouch. He did not know yet why.

Six cities behind them. Kael's bark was thick now, and its edges curled. He wrote the gold chapter twice and crossed one out, as if the first version had boasted.

Takshak had not appeared with a crown of snakes. He did not need to. The hopping back in the mine had been his handwriting.

The smiths taught Taro to hammer gold so thin you could see your breath through it. He made a disk for Borin. It was useless as armor, but a beautiful apology. Borin wore it under his shirt and never took it off.

At the stone table, the heat made them dizzy. The champion offered wine. Kael refused for all of them. "Clear heads," he said. The champion shrugged. "Clear heads break slower."

Borin's winning move was to leave one token in danger. Two of the champion's pieces had to chase it, and that opened a lane that Mani had been whispering for. Afterward, Borin could not lift his cup. Baby lifted it for him.

They came down through the clouds. Mani sang a meadow song, badly. Taro tried to sing along and failed beautifully.

Kael added a line to his record: *Gold buys sleep. We are not for sale.*

Highland children watched the six go down and shouted questions. Had they seen the serpent king? Had they eaten gold? Baby shouted back, "We ate hunger!" Mani gently hit his arm, smiling in spite of herself.

The tunnel that ate sound left Taro half deaf for a week. To make up for it, he began to touch people's shoulders before speaking to them. The habit stayed.

Gold dust got into their hair until they sparkled like small false suns. At a stream, they washed each other's hair like brothers and sisters, like survivors.

Four gates left, and the last one was the Crown.

After the game, the highland champion shook Borin's hand. "You play like a man who carries people in his pieces," he said. Borin could not speak. He only nodded.

Smoke from Suvarnagiri followed them down the pass for days, like a gray flag. Mani said it looked like a snake's breath. Kael said it looked like a forge remembering fire. Baby said it looked like home, if home were dangerous.

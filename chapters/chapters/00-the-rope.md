# Chapter 0: The Rope

**About 1000 BC · the reed gate at Vardhana**

Kael Gobu wrote this story when he was old. His hand shook. He wrote on a piece of tree bark. The bark smelled of smoke and ice.

His first line was this. "In the year the fields had no kingdom, six people left a reed town. They called the grass a road."

He did not start with the year. He started with the rope.

Ten families met at a bend in the river. The mud kept a footprint for only one day. The oldest people were too weak to travel. Still, they said the River Promise together, in one breath.

Six people must walk to the Crystal Crown at Sphatika. At each of ten gates, they must win the Royal Game.

If they won all ten, the ten families would become one people. The land would get a name. If they failed, the families would stay ten groups. They would keep fighting over water. An old power would keep pulling the world back into wild land.

Children knew that power only from stories by the fire. People called it the King of Snakes. They said he lived in the burned reed lands to the south. Later, his name was Takshak.

The old people said he could not enter a game after the die was thrown, if all six players stood in the court. Outside the game, he could do almost anything.

The reed gate at Vardhana was only two posts and a wet rope. Past it, the field went on and on. It looked as if nobody had ever chosen a path. Mist sat on the reeds. A tall bird stood on one leg and watched them. It looked as if the river was telling a joke.

Baby Dufaan held a wooden die in both hands. The die was older than the town. Someone had burned the dots in with a nail. When Baby turned it, the dots looked like small cuts that had healed badly.

Mani Dufaan was already on the other side of the rope.

"If you wait for the right number," she said, "the grass will grow over your feet."

Kael looked at the six of them. Taro Domizon had a copper pin between his teeth. He said it would become a tool before it became a weapon. Maya Mersace hid something in her sleeve. She had not shown it to anyone. Not even Mani. Mani usually knew everything about everyone. Borin Gubatti carried the bags. They had enough food for one season, if winter did not come too early.

They did not know they were starting a kingdom. They only knew the words the old people had given them. They felt ten families watching from the river bank.

Baby shook the die. It landed on six.

The number hit Kael in the chest like an order. Six dots. Six walkers. Not five. Not seven. The promise had teeth.

"Together," he said.

Mani did not wait. She walked through. The rope brushed her shoulder. It left a dark wet line on her shirt, as if the gate had written its name on her.

Baby looked at Kael. Kael looked at the others. Taro took the pin from his mouth and put it in his pocket. Maya stared at the far field. No one could read her face. Borin pulled his straps tight and followed Mani.

Baby crossed last. The die was still warm. When his foot touched the grass on the other side, he understood something without words. The road had started counting them.

Behind them, someone on the bank hit a reed drum once. It was not a party. It was a witness.

Ahead there was only a path, a rumor of ten cities, and a crown of crystal. No one in Vardhana had ever seen that crown.

Many years later, Kael would write that the rope was the real first gate. Everything after it asked one longer question. Could six people stay six?

Mani was already far ahead. She called back that the grass was shorter here. She said the snakes, if there were any, had not voted yet.

Baby ran to catch up.

Before the rope, at the river, an old woman had put the die in Baby's hands. "Wood remembers," she said. "Glass forgets. Iron lies." Baby did not know yet that he would carry this same piece of wood all the way to the crystal.

On the field side, Taro took the copper pin out again. "First tool of the walk," he said. Then Maya showed what was in her sleeve. It was not a weapon. It was a strip of bark. The ten family marks were already written on it. She had known they would need a reminder.

Borin moved the bags so the weight sat on his hips, not on his heart. "Six straps," he said. "Six lives."

Kael looked far ahead. He could not see any crown. There was only the job of trying.

The ten marks on Maya's bark shone in the light. Ten doors. Ten games. One long walk. The old people had said what would happen if the six failed at the ninth gate. Takshak would not burn them in one day. He would take the world apart slowly. Fields would turn back into reeds. Names would turn into wind. Winning was not about glory. It was like living with the weather.

Baby put the die in a small bag at his belt. The six dots faced out. Mani had already moved on. He thought a little luck might help.

The hundred years began.
